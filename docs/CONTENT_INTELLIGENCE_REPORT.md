# CONTENT INTELLIGENCE REPORT

**Date:** 2026-08-15 · **Scope:** Amazon KDP (rank 1) and its direct-sale companion rail

## 1. The reframe

The brief (§4) asks what readers "actually consume now" — high-performing categories, title patterns, hooks, length, emotional drivers. That framing suits a per-read platform where the goal is to win attention inside a feed. It is the wrong question for a marketplace, where the goal is to be **the best available answer to a query someone is already typing.**

The question I am answering instead: **where does high-intent demand currently outrun available supply?**

## 2. What the demand data actually says

| Signal | Value | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Self-published titles with ISBNs, 2025 | 3.5M+, **+38.7% YoY** | Supply is exploding |
| Authors citing discoverability as top problem | **78%** | Attention, not production, is scarce |
| Authors earning <$1,000/year | **75%** | The median outcome is failure |
| Authors with 1–3 books earning <$100/month | ~80% | Single-asset plays usually fail |
| Highest earners with an email list | **96%** (vs 53% of low earners) | Owned audience is the strongest correlate of income |
| Reported flooded genres | Monster Romance, M/M Romance, Reverse Harem, Dark Romance | Readers actively struggle to find human-authored work here |
| Reported high-earning AI niches | keto meal plans, day trading for beginners, dog training breed guides, journaling prompts | **Also the most imitated — these are lagging indicators** |

**The trap in that last row.** Every "high-earning AI niche" list is a description of where competitors already went. Entering a niche because it is documented as profitable means entering after the profit has been competed away. Those four niches are evergreen, which is precisely why thousands of AI titles already sit in them. Evergreen demand is stable *and* permanently contested.

## 3. The selection rule I derived instead

Compete on the one axis where a fast, research-capable, zero-marginal-cost publisher structurally beats both incumbents and slop farms: **recency**.

A topic whose governing facts changed 30 days ago cannot have a deep back catalogue. Traditional publishers are 9–18 months from print. Slop farms chase evergreen keyword lists and have no primary-source discipline, so they cannot write accurately about a rule that changed last week. The window is real but narrow — which is the point, because a narrow window is a window competitors cannot flood.

**Four tests a topic must pass:**

1. **High-intent** — money or legal exposure is at stake, so the buyer is deciding, not browsing.
2. **Recently changed** — governing facts moved within ~6 months.
3. **Primary-source verifiable** — every claim checkable through `WebSearch` against official sources, which is the one research channel this environment permits.
4. **Re-issuable** — the facts will move again, so the asset becomes a maintained edition line rather than a one-shot.

Test 3 is a capability constraint, not a preference. A strategy requiring Amazon BSR data or keyword volumes would be unsupportable here (egress blocked). This strategy is chosen to run on evidence I can actually obtain.

## 4. Validated opportunity: AI disclosure and transparency compliance

Verified 2026-08-15 against primary and near-primary sources:

| Rule | Effective | Substance |
|---|---|---|
| **EU AI Act Article 50** | **2026-08-02 (13 days ago)** | Binding across the EU. Synthetic audio/image/video/text must be machine-readably marked and detectable. Deepfakes and AI text on public-interest matters must be disclosed unless subject to substantive human editorial review with a person taking editorial responsibility. Penalties to **€15M or 3% of global turnover**. Pre-2026-08-02 content needs no retroactive labelling. Transitional relief on marking/detection for already-marketed systems runs to **2026-12-02**. |
| **Etsy templated-design rule** | **2026-08-11 (4 days ago)** | Computerised-tool items from templated designs disallowed unless the design is the seller's original work. Per-listing AI disclosure mandatory; listings missing it are **filtered from search**. Purchased or templated prompts violate policy. |
| **Amazon KDP** | Current | AI-*generated* text/images/translations must be disclosed; AI-*assisted* need not be. Undisclosed AI is a violation even where the content would have been permitted. Photorealistic AI people require the `contains-synthetic-performer` IPTC keyword in `dc:subject`. |
| **Medium** | Current | AI-generated writing cannot be paywalled, disclosed or not. Distribution tiering by disclosure status. |
| **YouTube** | Jan 2026 | Mandatory disclosure for synthetically generated media; mass-produced/templated uploads lose monetisation under the inauthentic content policy. |

**Why this passes all four tests:**

1. *High-intent* — an Etsy seller filtered out of search, or an EU business facing a €15M exposure ceiling, is not browsing.
2. *Recently changed* — the two most consequential rules are 13 and 4 days old.
3. *Primary-source verifiable* — every row above traces to an official source, and the research is already done as a by-product of Phase 0.
4. *Re-issuable* — the 2026-12-02 transitional deadline is a scheduled, pre-known trigger for a second edition.

**The supply gap.** Current coverage is a scatter of undated SEO blog posts that contradict each other on basic points such as whether AI-assisted work requires disclosure (it does not on KDP; it does on Etsy for images). Cross-platform, dated, primary-sourced synthesis does not appear to exist. That gap is the product.

## 5. Format and positioning decisions

| Decision | Value | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Format | Practical reference handbook, 12–18k words | Long enough for substance, short enough to be current on arrival. Reference beats narrative for a lookup-driven buyer |
| Price | **$9.99** | Top of the 70% band pre-expansion, ~$6.99/sale. High enough to signal seriousness, low enough for an impulse business expense |
| Edition dating | **On the cover and in metadata** | The strongest differentiator against undated blog content — and an honest signal of decay |
| Structure | Per-platform chapters + a cross-platform decision table | Buyers arrive with one platform in mind and discover they operate on three |
| Disclosure | Explicit AI-generated disclosure at KDP upload, plus an in-book note | Non-negotiable. A non-compliant book about compliance is a business-ending contradiction |
| Legal posture | Prominent not-legal-advice notice; every claim dated and cited | Duty of care on regulatory content |
| Exclusivity | **Non-exclusive** | Preserves the multi-platform asset strategy |

## 6. Prohibited patterns (editorial standard for this asset)

- No undated claims. Every rule carries its effective date and source.
- No "as of my knowledge" hedging, and no filling gaps from model memory — `UNKNOWN` is written as `UNKNOWN`.
- No legal advice framing. This is a compliance *reference*.
- No fabricated case studies, statistics or testimonials.
- No padding. A reference book is judged on lookup speed, and word-count inflation is the most recognisable AI-writing tell.
- No urgency-marketing or fear language beyond what the cited penalties factually state.
