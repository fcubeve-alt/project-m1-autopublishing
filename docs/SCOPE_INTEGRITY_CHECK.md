# SCOPE INTEGRITY CHECK — E1 and E2 against the original economic model

**Date:** 2026-08-15 · **Trigger:** Owner instruction to check E1/E2 against the *original* model, not the broader "AI writing income" category.

---

## 1. The classification

| Class | Definition | The test that distinguishes it |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Platform directly compensates creators for reads / content / royalties / contests / tips, **using its own existing audience** | *Does the platform send me money out of an audience it already owns?* |
| **B** | Audience-acquisition platform; **not itself the monetisation engine** | *Do I have to leave the platform to get paid?* |
| **C** | Retail publishing marketplace — sells work to buyers, pays royalties | *Does a buyer make a purchase decision?* |

---

## 2. E1 and E2 classified

### E1 — Royal Road serial → **CLASS B. Not an income experiment.**

**Royal Road does not pay authors.** There is no per-read payment, no revenue share, no contest fund. Every dollar arrives elsewhere — Patreon, KDP, direct sales. Our own rules matrix has recorded this correctly all along: *"Indirect — audience to Patreon/KDP."*

**I let "easy to start" masquerade as "fits the model."** The reasoning that promoted E1 to *primary* was: no KYC → unblocked by H1 → signal in days. Every one of those is true, and none of them is a reason to call it an income experiment. Operational convenience is not economic fit.

**Corrected status: ACQUISITION / LEARNING PROBE.** It remains genuinely valuable — it is the cheapest way to resolve the biggest open question in the landscape (*can disclosed AI fiction hold readers?*), for which no public data exists. But it is now labelled honestly and **must not be counted toward the Mission's revenue objective.**

### E2 — KDP Short Read → **CLASS C. In scope, and now the primary income experiment.**

Royalties are explicitly named in the seed model, and KDP is a third-party publishing platform with its own distribution. It qualifies.

Honest caveat: **C is not the Vocal pattern.** A retail buyer pays per copy; Vocal pays per read from its own audience. Related, and inside the stated wording — but the Owner should know that promoting C to primary means the experiment's centre of gravity has moved from the seed example's class to an adjacent one.

### Neither E1 nor E2 is Class A — and Class A is the mission's core

That is the real finding of this check.

**Verified open Class A platforms: two.** Vocal (economics fail at our scale, plus a country hard-block) and Tapas (audience gate). Everything else is closed, dead, or unresolved.

**The pattern is not incidental.** Platforms paying creators from their own audience bear the cost of every marginal piece of content, so unlimited zero-marginal-cost supply is an existential threat to them. They have responded by barring or de-monetising it. Retail marketplaces bear no such cost — the buyer pays per copy — which is precisely why Class C stayed open. **The economics of each class predicted its AI policy.**

## 4. Proposed scope decision — for the Owner, not enacted by me

The Mission Scope may be economically constrained in a way that was not knowable when the experiment was designed. Per instruction, I am **proving it with evidence and proposing**, not silently changing anything.

**The finding:** the pure Class A model — AI-written content earning directly from a platform's existing audience — has only two verified-open venues in 2026, and both fail our constraints (Vocal on economics and Stripe; Tapas on its audience gate). Vocal, the seed example, is verified open *and* verified unviable at our scale.

**Options, with my recommendation:**

| Option | What it means | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Conclude the experiment on the finding** | The answer to "can AI writing earn via audience-owning platforms?" is *largely no, in 2026* — a real and valuable result | Intellectually honest; produces no revenue |
| **2. ⭐ Continue via Class C (recommended)** | Royalties are named in the model. Run KDP as the income experiment, with Royal Road (B) as a free acquisition probe | Stays inside the stated wording; only route with a plausible path to real revenue |
| **3. Test the Class A residue** | Resolve Simily and the AnyStories cluster; if any is genuinely open, run a small Vocal/Simily probe | Cheap, but the ceiling is low and Stripe/country risk is unresolved |
| **4. Widen the scope** | Admit adjacent models | **Not recommended** — this is the drift already corrected twice |

**My recommendation: Option 2, with Option 3 running alongside at low cost**, because Option 3 is what would falsify my own conclusion. I would rather actively hunt evidence against my finding than defend it.

**I am proceeding on Option 2 + 3 as the working assumption** — both sit inside the stated scope, so neither requires a scope change. If the Owner prefers Option 1, say so and I will stop and write the conclusion instead.
