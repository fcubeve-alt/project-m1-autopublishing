# PLATFORM TOP 5 — Ranked Selection

**Date:** 2026-08-15 · **Basis:** `PLATFORM_LONG_LIST.md`, `PLATFORM_RULES_MATRIX.csv`

## The selection logic, stated before the ranking

The brief asked for a Top 5 chosen on "evidence and economics, not fame." Applying that honestly produces an uncomfortable result: **this is not a list of five comparable options.** It is one viable Phase-1 channel, three deliberate follow-ons that depend on the first one working, and one hedge.

Presenting five co-equal platforms would misrepresent the evidence. The ranking below is weighted by a single question: *does this platform supply demand, or does it require me to bring demand?* We have no audience and no ad budget. A platform that requires us to bring traffic contributes nothing at Phase 1, regardless of how good its margins look on paper.

**Scoring weights used:** built-in demand ×3 · AI content permitted ×3 · payout feasibility ×2 · time-to-first-revenue ×2 · margin ×2 · compliance risk ×−2 · audience prerequisite ×−3.

---

## Rank 1 — Amazon KDP (eBook, wide / non-exclusive) — score 9/10

**Why it wins:** it is the only platform in the entire 43-platform survey that simultaneously (a) puts an unknown seller in front of buyers with purchase intent at zero cost, (b) expressly permits AI-generated content subject to disclosure, and (c) pays real money per unit rather than fractions of a cent per view.

**Verified economics:** 70% royalty band is **$2.99–$12.99 as of 2026-07-07** (widened from $2.99–$9.99). At $9.99 that is ~$6.99 per sale. To clear a $300 first-revenue target requires ~43 sales — a plausible number for a well-targeted title, and a target that would require roughly 60,000+ Medium member-reading views to match, on a platform that forbids us anyway.

**Key decision — go wide, not KDP Select.** KDP Select demands exclusivity in exchange for Kindle Unlimited page reads at ~$0.00482/normalised page from a fund that is diluting as the catalogue grows. Exclusivity would void the brief's §8 multi-platform content-asset strategy for the sake of a shrinking per-page pool. **Non-exclusive is the correct call**, and it keeps ranks 2–4 open.

**Risks, priced honestly:** 75% of self-published authors earn under $1,000/year and 78% cite discoverability as their main problem. A title that does not answer a real query is invisible. This is why the content strategy is timing-based rather than volume-based — see `CONTENT_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md`.

**Human gate:** account creation, tax interview, bank details. Irreducible.

## Rank 2 — Direct sale rail: Gumroad / Payhip / Ko-fi — score 7/10 *(as a companion, not a source of demand)*

**Role:** captures full margin (~90–95% vs 70%) from any traffic we generate ourselves, and — critically — is the **fallback if the Owner's country is poorly served by Amazon's payout options**. Ko-fi in particular supports PayPal-only payout where Stripe is unavailable, which matters because the Owner's payout geography is currently `UNKNOWN`.

**Why not rank 1:** it supplies zero demand. Selling here requires an audience we do not have. Standing alone it is a store with no street.

**Selection within the rail** is deferred until the Owner's country is known — the three differ mainly in payout coverage, which is exactly the unresolved variable.

---

## Rank 3 — Wide retail via Draft2Digital (Kobo / Apple / B&N and 30+ others) — score 6/10

**Role:** one integration fans the same asset out to Apple Books (70%), Kobo (70% at $2.99+), and libraries. Pure incremental revenue on an asset whose production cost is already sunk.

**Timing:** deliberately deferred to day 15+ of the 30-day plan. D2D now charges **$20 to open an account** and **$12/year maintenance on titles earning under $100/12 months** — explicitly to deter AI content farms. That is real money and a real signal: pay it once Amazon has demonstrated the asset sells, not before. Spending $20 to distribute an unvalidated asset is exactly the "automate before proving the loop" error the brief warns against.

---

## Rank 4 — Substack or beehiiv — score 5/10 *(deferred by design, not by doubt)*

**Role:** the correct long-term home for realised margin — ~85–87% retained on Substack, with payouts reaching the bank in ~48 hours versus Amazon's ~90-day lag. It is where this business should end up.

**Why it is not the starting point:** it converts an existing audience into recurring revenue. It does not create an audience. Starting here means writing to nobody for months with no feedback signal — the opposite of a short experiment cycle. It activates once Asset A0001 has produced buyers who can be invited onto a list.

*(beehiiv note: Email Boosts and Direct Links were deprecated 2026-04-10; Ad Network access requires the Scale plan or above. Substack is the better zero-cost entry of the two.)*

---

## Rank 5 — Royal Road — score 3/10 *(hedge only, and a weak one)*

**Role:** the only meaningful **fiction** route where AI-generated content is expressly permitted rather than banned, under a clear three-tier tagging policy.

**Why it ranks last despite being permitted:** the tag is a commercial penalty. Royal Road's own forums document sustained reader backlash against AI-tagged works. The policy grants legal permission and the audience withholds commercial permission. We would be compliant and unread.

**Held open because** it is the designated test bed if the Owner supplies a fiction Creative Seed (brief §5B). It is not part of the 30-day plan.

## The honest summary

**Phase 1 is a one-platform bet on Amazon KDP, wide/non-exclusive, with a direct-sale rail as payout insurance.** Ranks 3–5 are sequenced consequences of rank 1 succeeding, not parallel options.

Diversifying across five platforms before any one of them has produced a single sale would spread a fixed effort budget across five unvalidated hypotheses and slow every feedback loop. The brief's own §19 governs here: **first prove the money loop, then extend it.**
