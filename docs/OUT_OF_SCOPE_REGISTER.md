# OUT-OF-SCOPE REGISTER

**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Purpose:** preserve genuine findings that fall outside **this** experiment's boundary, so they are neither lost nor allowed to consume this mission's research budget.

**This experiment's boundary:** AI produces English written content → published via third-party platforms that already have an audience or distribution mechanism → earning through reads, revenue share, publication payments, contests, royalties or tips.

**These are not substitutes for completing that mission.** They are handed to the broader Money OS for separate evaluation.

## O1 — Freelance writing services (Fiverr / Upwork)

**Why out of scope:** selling labour, not publishing content through an audience-owning platform. The buyer commissions; there is no distribution mechanism earning us reads or royalties.

**Findings worth keeping:**
- **Fiverr [Official]:** AI-generated or AI-assisted work is permitted but must be high-quality, original and meaningfully refined to the client's requirements. Generic, unmodified or reused AI output fails quality standards. **Sellers need not disclose AI use in the gig description** — only if a client asks, or to honour an explicit "no AI" request made at or before order start.
- **Upwork [Reported]:** permits AI-assisted proposals, prohibits fully automated submission; reported to use ~5 detection layers on proposals but **not** to scan deliverables. From 2026-01-05, accounts are opted in by default to AI training on new work product and messages unless changed in AI Preferences.
- **Economics [Reported]:** entry-level median ~$35/hr; Upwork takes 10–20%, Fiverr 20%. New-seller income near zero for the first 4–8 weeks; first 90 days are "proof, not profit."

**Assessment for Money OS:** genuinely proven buyer intent — the strongest of anything surveyed. Fails *this* experiment on scope and on the 2–3h/month attention budget. **Would rank first if the constraint were revenue certainty rather than autonomous operation.**

## O2 — Digital templates (Notion / Gumroad / Etsy)

**Why out of scope:** a template is a tool, not written content, and the primary rails supply no audience.

**Findings worth keeping [Reported]:**
- Gumroad ~146,000 products; templates a major segment of a Design category estimated at ~$8.8M. Top sellers ~$3,200/month. On a $19 template the creator keeps ~$16.13 (~85% vs KDP's 70%).
- Notion Marketplace "sends buyers to creators with zero followers" but is **supplementary, not primary** for discovery; approval queue; 10% + $0.40.
- **Functional templates consistently outperform aesthetic ones.**
- Strongest niches: profession-specific workflow systems (therapists, photographers, real-estate agents) and life administration. Generic "life OS"/"second brain" is the most competitive category.

**Transferable insight:** *functional beats decorative, and specific beats generic* — a content principle that survives the scope boundary and should inform Stage 4.

## O3 — Micro-SaaS / API products

**Why out of scope:** software, not written content.

**Findings [Reported]:** 6–12 months to first paying customer is typical; the field's own consensus is that building is solved and **distribution is the bottleneck** — the same wall as everything else, with a longer runway.

## O4 — Professional compliance tooling (paused asset A0002)

**Why out of scope:** this is the drift the scope correction caught. A0002 — a California AB 723 listing-photo disclosure workbook for real estate agents — is a professional compliance product sold as a book. It uses a publishing platform, but the *content* is professional tooling rather than articles, stories, fiction or comparable written work, and its selection logic came from "which profession has budget," not from this experiment's question.

**Status: PAUSED, not killed.** The research remains verified and reusable:
- **California AB 723 → Bus. & Prof. Code §10140.8, effective 2026-01-01 [Official].** Brokers, salespersons and persons acting on their behalf must disclose digitally altered listing images and provide a link/URL/QR code to the original unaltered image, reasonably conspicuous and on or adjacent to the image. Precise statutory definition, with an explicit carve-out for lighting, sharpening, white balance, colour correction, angle, straightening, cropping and exposure.
- Non-disclosure reported as a **criminal offence** rather than a board fine **[Reported]**.
- MLS penalties reported at $500–$5,000 plus listing removal; NAR Article 12 / SoP 12-5 apply **[Reported]**.

**Handed to Money OS** as a standalone candidate. If Money OS wants a professional-compliance product line, this is a verified, dated, high-stakes opportunity ready to build.

## O5 — EU AI Act correction (carries across all scopes)

**[Reported]** EU AI Act **Annex III high-risk obligations — including recruitment and HR — moved to 2 December 2027** by the Digital Omnibus provisional agreement of 7 May 2026, not August 2026 as widely assumed. Annex I embedded products move to 2 August 2028.

**Article 50 transparency obligations are unaffected and have applied since 2 August 2026.**

Worth keeping because the widely-repeated version of this timeline is wrong, and it materially changes urgency for any HR/recruitment-facing product in any scope.
