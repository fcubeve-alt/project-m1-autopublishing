# A0001 — Buyer Intent Analysis

**Date:** 2026-08-15 · **Required by:** `OPERATING_RULES.md` R4
**Question forced by the Reviewer:** why would anyone pay $6.99 for information Google gives away free?

**Short answer: I cannot prove they would.** What follows is the honest analysis and what I am doing about it.

---

## 1. What I have, and what I don't

| Evidence type | Status |
|---|---|
| **Need exists** | **Strong.** EU AI Act Art. 50 binding 2026-08-02 with penalties to €15M or 3% turnover; Etsy changes days old; KDP, Medium, YouTube, Wattpad, Royal Road, Ko-fi rules all differ and several are actively misreported |
| **Confusion exists** | **Strong, and self-demonstrated.** Compiling this book surfaced three widely repeated claims that the platforms' own material contradicts |
| **People search for this** | Reasonable inference from the volume of SEO content targeting these queries. Not measured — `UNRATED` |
| **People *pay* for this** | **NOT ESTABLISHED.** No BSR data, no keyword volume, no competing-title sales evidence. Searches for category purchase evidence returned nothing usable |

The Reviewer's distinction is exactly right and I ran the two together in the first pass. **"Rules recently changed" is need evidence. It says nothing about willingness to pay.** Someone Googling "YouTube AI disclosure rules" is not thereby a buyer of a 40-page Kindle handbook.

I also cannot close this gap by research: Amazon marketplace data is unreachable from this environment, and scraping it would breach Amazon's terms, which `COMPLIANCE_POLICY.md` forbids. So this is not a gap I can research my way out of — only one I can test my way out of.

---

## 2. Applying the paid-beats-free test (R4)

A paid product must win on at least one of four structural grounds. Scored honestly:

| Ground | A0001 as originally drafted | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Artifact** — buyer receives something usable, not just an explanation | Explanatory prose with checklists buried at chapter ends | ❌ **Fails** |
| **2. Aggregation cost** — assembling from free sources costs more than the price | True in fact (7 platforms + EU law, ~15 primary-source queries, 3 corrections found). But **the buyer cannot know this before paying** | ⚠️ **Real but unprovable at point of sale** |
| **3. Accountability** — dated, sourced, citable where free content is not | Every claim carries an effective date and an evidence grade. Genuinely differentiated from undated SEO posts | ✅ **Passes** |
| **4. Budget context** — bought with business money where price signals seriousness | $6.99 reads as consumer impulse, not professional reference | ❌ **Fails** |

**One clear pass, one unprovable, two failures.** That is a marginal paid product, and the Reviewer is right to withhold approval. A product that competes head-on with free blog content on the same ground will lose.

---

## 3. The fix — and the mechanism that makes it work

The weakness is **format, not topic**. Three changes, all cheap, all made:

**(a) Lead with the artifact.** Move the checklists and the disclosure-record template to the front as a usable compliance toolkit; demote the explanatory chapters to reference material behind them. The buyer should receive something they *fill in*, not only something they read.

**(b) Put the differentiator inside the free sample.** This is the important one. Ground 2 fails only because the buyer cannot assess aggregation value before paying — so **move the proof to where they can see it for free.** Amazon's "Look Inside" exposes roughly the first 10%. Placing the evidence-grading key, the cross-platform decision table, and the **corrections table** (three claims the platforms' own material contradicts) inside that window converts an unverifiable claim into a pre-purchase signal. A reader who sees "here are three things everyone tells you that are wrong, with sources" has been shown the value rather than promised it.

**(c) Hold price at $6.99.** Raising price to fix ground 4 would be optimising a signal on unproven demand. Price moves after data, not before.

---

## 4. Decision

**A0001 proceeds — reclassified from "validated opportunity" to "demand probe."**

The reasoning, stated plainly so it can be argued with:

1. Purchase intent **cannot** be established in advance from this environment. That is a fact about the constraint, not an excuse.
2. When information cannot be bought with research, buy it with the cheapest possible experiment. The test costs **$0** and returns a signal in 30 days.
3. Every alternative in `OPPORTUNITY_COMPARISON.md` has *equally* unproven intent for our specific execution, at higher cost or longer time-to-signal — except KDP genre fiction, which has proven buyer intent but needs 30–90 days and a series.
4. The probe's real product is **information about whether the recency thesis works at all** — which governs the entire strategy, not just this asset.

**What this classification changes:**

- Not described as validated in any status report.
- **No scaling on hope.** No second title, no Draft2Digital $20, no derivatives until it returns a signal.
- Failure is a **valid, useful outcome** — it falsifies the recency thesis for ~$0 and redirects C2 to KDP genre fiction on its proven intent.

**Reversal trigger:** if the Owner's editorial review finds the topic unsuitable, or if a stronger candidate with *proven* purchase intent appears before publication, A0001 is replaced without hesitation. It is a probe, not a commitment.

---

## 5. What I got wrong, stated plainly

I found a clever thesis — recency beats volume — and let its elegance substitute for demand evidence. The thesis explains why *competition would be thin*. It never addressed whether *anyone would buy*. Those are different questions and I answered only the first while writing as though I had answered both.

`OPERATING_RULES.md` R4 exists so that the next cycle asks the second question before committing, not after a Reviewer forces it.
