## The trigger that actually matters

Individual policy changes are noise. **The signal that would reopen this mission is a change in who bears marginal cost.**

Class A closed because platforms paying creators from their own audience absorb the cost of every marginal item, making unlimited zero-cost supply an existential threat. That logic reverses only if:

1. **A platform makes AI supply its own advantage** — e.g. paying for disclosed AI content because it has solved ranking or has a reader segment that wants it; or
2. **Reader-side willingness to pay for disclosed AI work becomes visible** — turning the tag from a penalty into a neutral or positive label; or
3. **A new Class A platform launches AI-native**, with economics designed around zero marginal cost rather than defending against it.

**Watch for those three.** Do not re-litigate individual tag rules — they are downstream of the economics.

## Review protocol

- **Cadence:** quarterly. **Next review: 2026-11-15.**
- **Budget:** ≤3 searches. Prioritise W1 and W3 (the only items where a change would plausibly alter the verdict).
- **Escalation:** if any watched item changes such that a Class A platform becomes open *and* economically viable *and* payout-accessible, **do not resume M-A silently.** Write a new charter and make it compete (`OPERATING_RULES.md` R10).
- **Retirement:** if two consecutive reviews show no movement on W1–W4, drop cadence to annual. The finding is structural; checking it often is theatre.
