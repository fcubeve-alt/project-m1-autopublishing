## 1. What P-0 asked

> Is there a zero-cost route to put a paid reference in front of a defined professional audience, given no existing audience, no ad budget, and ~2–3 hours of Owner attention per month?

M-P was chartered specifically because this question could be answered **before** writing anything. It was designed to try to kill itself first. It did.

## 2. What P-0 found

### Finding 1 — The community route fails the attention constraint

Reddit and professional forums operate on a **90/10 rule** (90% genuine participation, 10% promotion), and many high-value professional subreddits enforce closer to **99/1**, removing contributions from anyone with promotional history. Disclosure of affiliation is required. **[R]**

This is not a zero-cost channel. It is a **sustained-human-presence** channel, priced in exactly the resource we have least of. Against 2–3 hours per month it is unusable.

### Finding 2 — The decisive one: professional compliance knowledge is an *institutional* purchase, not a discretionary one

Taking real estate — the profession with our strongest verified hook (CA AB 723) — the actual pipeline is: **[R]**

| Channel | Nature |
|---|---|
| State regulatory agency (e.g. CA DRE) | **Free and authoritative** — publishes the requirements directly |
| **State-approved CE providers** (McKissock, 360training, Champions, firsttuesday) | Courses must be **state-approved to count** toward licence renewal |
| NAR and affiliated institutes | Association-supplied |
| **Brokerages** | Frequently provide or subsidise CE for their agents |

The consequence is fatal to the thesis. An unapproved third-party reference would compete against:

1. **A free authoritative source** — the regulator publishes the rule itself;
2. **Mandatory, often employer-subsidised, state-approved education** the agent must take regardless; and
3. A **regulatory approval barrier** to entering the real channel — state course approval is an administrative process, not a writing problem, and cannot be cleared at $0 with 2–3 Owner hours.

**High need + high budget + real legal exposure — and still no discretionary purchase**, because the need is already met by an institutional pipeline.

### Finding 3 — M-P had no distinct reachability advantage

Stripping out the routes above, the only remaining zero-cost channel that supplies traffic is **Amazon marketplace search** — which is **M-C's channel**.

So M-P was never a different distribution bet. It was a different *product* on the same channel, and therefore carried the same H1 dependency and the same discoverability uncertainty.

## 3. Why this is a kill, not a downgrade

The charter decision rested on one load-bearing claim: *M-P can be killed before production because it has a distinct, cheaply-testable route to a paying professional audience.*

**P-0 falsified that claim.** There is no distinct route, and the audience it named is served institutionally.

A literal reading might let M-P survive — a zero-cost route does technically exist (Amazon). I am rejecting that reading. The criterion existed to test whether M-P had a *reachability advantage worth chartering it over M-C for*. It does not. Surviving on a technicality after the justifying premise has been falsified is precisely the mission-stretching R10 forbids.

**Pre-committed trigger applied as written:** *"P-0 fails → M-P dies immediately. Charter M-C."*

## 4. What this did NOT disprove

- **Not disproven: the research capability.** The primary-source edge is real — three documented corrections and a verified dated statute stand. What failed is the *business model built on it*, not the capability. It remains available to any future mission.
- **Not disproven: professionals buy books.** What P-0 found is narrower and sharper: for **compliance and licensing knowledge specifically**, an institutional pipeline already serves the need.
- **Not concluded: other professions.** But the finding is likely to generalise wherever compliance knowledge is **licensed, mandatory, or employer-funded** — which is most regulated professions. That is a broad exclusion, and it is the reason this is a kill rather than a niche change.

## 6. Durable lesson

> **A need that is already met institutionally is not a market — regardless of how acute the need, how large the budget, or how real the legal exposure.**

Before assuming discretionary purchase from a professional audience, check whether the need is served by: a free authoritative source (regulators publish their own rules) · mandatory licensed education · an employer-funded channel · or a channel with an approval barrier we cannot pass.
