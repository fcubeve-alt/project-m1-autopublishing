## 1. The question this mission was built to answer

> Can AI-produced English written content earn real external revenue by publishing on third-party platforms that already have readers, and that **compensate creators directly out of their own audience** — via reads, revenue share, publication payments, contests or tips?

Vocal Media was the seed example of the pattern, not a predetermined winner.

## 2. The answer

**No — not in 2026, not at meaningful scale, and not for a publisher without an existing audience.**

Stated precisely, so it can be argued with:

> Of the Class A platforms screened, **seven are verified closed** to substantially-AI-generated work, **three are dead or closed to new entrants**, and **exactly two are verified open** — both with conditions that defeat our constraints. The residue is either work-for-hire (out of scope), contract-hazardous, or unverifiable.

## 4. Why the answer is structural, not incidental

The single most transferable insight from this mission:

> **A platform's cost structure predicts its AI policy.**
>
> Platforms that pay creators from their own audience bear the cost of every marginal item published. Unlimited zero-marginal-cost supply is therefore an existential threat, and they have responded by barring or de-monetising it. Retail marketplaces bear no such cost — the buyer pays per copy — which is exactly why that class stayed open.

This means the Class A closure is **not a temporary policy fashion**. It follows from the economics of the business model, so it is unlikely to reverse while generation costs stay near zero. Anyone re-testing this should look for a *change in who bears marginal cost*, not for a change in sentiment.
