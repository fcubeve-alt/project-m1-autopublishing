## Reading the table

**M-S (services) has the strongest buyer-intent evidence of anything surveyed** — and fails the Mission's autonomy dimension outright. It converts the Owner into the product and consumes attention we do not have. It is a good business and the wrong business for this system. *Reversal condition: Owner attention rises to ~5h/week.*

**M-T (templates) has no distribution.** Notion Marketplace is "supplementary, not primary" **[R]**; Gumroad supplies zero traffic. Its two findings — *functional beats aesthetic, specific beats generic* — are more valuable than the family itself, and they transfer.

**M-B (audience-first) is the correct long-term destination and the wrong starting point.** 96% of top-earning indie authors have an email list **[R]**. That is where this should end up, not where it can begin.

**M-C is open but crowded**, with a median outcome of failure and a structural dependence on catalogue scale a first experiment cannot supply. Its main attraction is that we already have a nearly-finished asset — which R7 explicitly forbids as a reason.

**M-P is the one family where we have a demonstrated, specific edge.** During M-A, primary-source research produced three documented corrections to claims repeated confidently across the free web, and surfaced a dated statutory obligation (CA AB 723, effective 2026-01-01, reported criminal exposure) that most affected professionals have not read. That capability is:

- **Real and demonstrated**, not hypothesised
- **Aligned with what buyers with budgets actually pay for** — passing all four paid-beats-free grounds where A0001 passed one
- **Renewable** — rules keep changing, creating a maintained edition line rather than a one-shot

## Recommendation

**Charter M-P next, as a small, pre-killed research mission. Hold M-C as the fallback.**

Reasoning, stated so it can be attacked:

1. **M-P is the only family where we have an identified, evidence-backed advantage.** Everywhere else we are an undifferentiated entrant competing on volume — the losing position M-A already diagnosed.
2. **It shares M-C's channel economics** (marketplace or direct sale, disclosure-neutral, $0 entry) **without inheriting M-C's core weakness** — it does not depend on catalogue scale, because a single authoritative reference in a narrow niche is a complete product.
3. **The buyer profile fixes what killed A0001** — professionals who expense purchases, versus general creators who are the most free-content-served segment available.
4. **It is cheap to falsify.** Its central question is *"will professionals pay for a dated, sourced compliance reference?"* — answerable with one small product, not a catalogue.

**Honest counter-argument, recorded rather than buried:** M-P is regulatory-adjacent, which raises duty of care (R5) and means a legal-interpretation error is more costly than a bad chapter. That is a real risk. It is manageable — the three-tier separation (Official Rule / Interpretation / Recommended Practice) already exists and was built for exactly this — but it must be a stated condition of chartering, not an afterthought.

**Also honest:** M-P looks attractive partly *because* we did the AB 723 research already. That is the same sunk-cost pull that R7 warns about. The difference — and I think it holds — is that the argument above rests on a **capability** we demonstrated (primary-source research speed on fast-moving rules), not on an **artifact** we happen to possess. If the capability claim is wrong, M-P should lose.

## Next step
