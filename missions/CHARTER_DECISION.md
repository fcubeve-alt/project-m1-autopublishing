# CHARTER DECISION — M-P vs M-C

**Date:** 2026-08-15 · **Method:** counterfactual test (R7) applied to both, from zero.

---

## The test

> If I had invested nothing in either, knowing everything I know today, which is the best use of the next unit of time, compute, money and Owner attention?

## Head to head

| Dimension | **M-C** Retail marketplaces | **M-P** Professional compliance products |
|---|---|---|
| Central question | Can a no-audience, no-catalogue publisher achieve organic marketplace discovery? | Will exposed professionals pay for a dated, primary-sourced reference? |
| **Our edge** | **None specific.** Undifferentiated entrant among 3.5M+ titles/yr | **Demonstrated** — primary-source speed on fast-changing rules; 3 documented corrections |
| Median outcome (known) | **Failure** — 75% earn <$1,000/yr; ~80% with 1–3 books earn <$100/mo | `[U]` |
| Structural weakness | **Depends on catalogue scale**, which a first experiment cannot have | **Narrow audience ceiling**; reachability unproven |
| Cheapest kill | After production + 30 days listing | **Before production** — P-0 tests reachability at ~$0 |
| Unit economics | $4.89/sale at $6.99 | $14–$35/sale at $19.99–$49 |
| Blocking gate | H1 | H1 only if marketplace channel |
| Duty of care | Normal | **Elevated** — regulatory content |

## Decision: charter **M-P**. Hold **M-C** as documented fallback.

**Four reasons, in order of weight:**

**1. M-P can be killed before production; M-C cannot.** This is decisive and it is not a tiebreaker. M-C requires writing a full-length work *before* learning whether organic discovery exists. M-P's most likely failure — *can we reach these people at all?* — is answerable at near-zero cost **before a word is written**. Given that the mission-level goal is finding truth cheaply, the family with the cheaper kill wins on the metric that matters.

**2. M-C's known weakness is structural and unfixable at experiment scale.** Catalogue size is the strongest income predictor; cozy mystery runs on series read-through; a first experiment has a catalogue of one. To give M-C a fair test we would have to commit to a *catalogue*, which is precisely the substantial resource commitment this comparison exists to prevent.

**3. M-P is the only family with a demonstrated edge.** Everywhere else we are an undifferentiated entrant competing on volume — the losing position M-A already diagnosed. The edge here was *shown*, not asserted: three claims repeated across the free web turned out to be wrong when checked, and a dated statutory obligation surfaced that most affected professionals have not read.

**4. M-P fixes the specific failure that killed A0001.** A0001 passed one of four paid-beats-free grounds. M-P's form addresses both failures directly: buyers who **expense** purchases (budget context) and a product that is a **working tool** (artifact).

## What would reverse this

Pre-committed, so it is not rationalised later:

| Trigger | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **P-0 fails** — no zero-cost route to a professional audience | **M-P dies immediately.** Charter M-C |
| Owner declines regulatory-adjacent content | M-P dies. Charter M-C |
| H1 reveals a payout geography that blocks direct rails but not Amazon | Re-run this comparison — it would shift the channel logic toward M-C |
| The edge fails to replicate — a new topic yields no `[Official]`-grade corrections | The core claim is false. Kill M-P; do **not** iterate |

## Honest self-check

**Am I picking M-P because we already did the AB 723 research?** That pull is real and R10 names it. Two things persuade me the reasoning holds:

- The argument rests on a **capability** (primary-source research speed) rather than an **artifact** (the AB 723 file). If the capability does not replicate on a *new* topic, the reversal table above kills the mission.
- **The profession is not pre-decided.** AB 723 was selected under a drifted scope. M-P's charter explicitly requires re-justifying the audience from scratch — so the existing research is not permitted to choose the niche.

**Am I under-rating M-C?** Possibly. Its channel is genuinely the best distribution available to an unknown seller, and that is not a small thing. It is held as fallback rather than killed for exactly that reason.

## Immediate next action

Run **P-0** — the reachability test — before any production:

> Is there a zero-cost route to put a paid reference in front of a defined professional audience, given no existing audience, no ad budget, and an egress-limited research environment?

If P-0 fails, M-P dies cheaply and M-C is chartered. That is the intended design: **the first thing this mission does is try to kill itself.**
