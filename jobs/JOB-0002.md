## The opportunity, verified 2026-08-15

**California AB 723 → Business & Professions Code §10140.8, effective 1 January 2026.** First-in-the-nation law, signed 10 October 2025.

| Element | Substance | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | Licensed real estate brokers and salespersons, **and persons acting on their behalf** | **[Official]** — bill text |
| **Trigger** | Including a *digitally altered image* in an advertisement or promotional material for the sale of real property | **[Official]** |
| **Required** | (1) A statement disclosing the image has been altered, **and** (2) a link, URL or QR code to a publicly accessible site clearly identifying the **original, unaltered image** | **[Official]** |
| **Placement** | Statement must be **reasonably conspicuous** and **on or adjacent to the image**, and must indicate the unaltered images can be accessed via the link | **[Official]** |
| **"Digitally altered"** | Altered via photo editing software **or AI** to add, remove or change elements — including fixtures, furniture, appliances, flooring, walls, paint colour, hardscape, landscape, facade, floor plans, and elements outside of or visible from the property | **[Official]** |
| **Carve-out** | **Does NOT include** lighting, sharpening, white balance, colour correction, angle, straightening, cropping, exposure, or other common adjustments that do not change the representation of the property | **[Official]** |
| **Consequence** | Reported as a **criminal offence** under Real Estate Law rather than a board fine | **[Reported]** — verify exact penalty against the code before asserting |

**The MLS/NAR layer, which stacks on top:**

- NAR Article 12 requires REALTORS® to "present a true picture" in advertising; Standard of Practice 12-5 extends this to digital images **[Reported]**
- NAR virtual staging guidance calls for captions such as "Virtually Staged" on enhanced photos **[Reported]**
- MLS penalties reported at **$500–$5,000**, plus listing removal **[Reported]**
- Originals must be retained and made available on request **[Reported]**

| **Accountability** | ✅ | ✅ inherited — same evidence grading |
| **Budget context** | ❌ $6.99 consumer | ✅ licensed professionals who expense marketing/compliance tools, facing criminal exposure |

**The decisive feature is the carve-out line.** The statute distinguishes *changing the representation* (covered) from *common adjustments* (exempt). Colour correction is exempt; removing a bin is not. Straightening is exempt; adding furniture is not. Every listing photo is a judgement against that line — which is precisely what a decision tree solves and prose does not. **That is the artifact.**

The second feature is the **QR/URL-to-original requirement**, which is not an information problem at all — it is a *workflow* problem. Agents must host originals somewhere public, map each altered image to its original, and keep it live. Nobody's blog post solves that. A worksheet does.

## Structure — worksheets first, reference behind

1. **The 60-second test** — is this photo covered? One-page decision tree

6. **The photo provenance log** — the fill-in record (who altered, what changed, which tool, where the original lives)
7. **The MLS layer** — NAR Article 12 / SoP 12-5, virtual staging captions, retention, penalty exposure

9. **Beyond California** — what to watch as other states follow; how to build a workflow that survives the next statute
10. **Pre-publication checklist** — per listing, before it goes live
11. Sources, dated

## Editorial standard — raised, because the stakes are

This asset concerns conduct reported to carry **criminal** consequence. `OPERATING_RULES.md` R5 applies at full strength.

- **Three tiers kept visually distinct throughout:** `Official Rule` / `[Interpretation]` / `[Recommended Practice]`. No interpretation may wear the statute's clothing.
- **Quote the statutory definition and carve-out verbatim.** Paraphrase loses the distinction that the whole book turns on.
- **Verify the penalty characterisation** against the code before publication. "Criminal offence" is currently `[Reported]`; if it cannot be confirmed primary, state it as reported and do not sharpen it.
- **Every claim dated and graded.** `[Unknown]` written as `[Unknown]`.
- **Prominent front-matter notice:** not legal advice; jurisdiction-specific; verify with the broker, the MLS, and counsel. Given criminal exposure this must be genuinely prominent, not boilerplate.
- **MLS rules vary by board.** Never assert a specific MLS's rule without a source; direct the reader to their own board and give them the questions to ask.
- **No fear marketing.** The cited penalties are alarming enough stated plainly; anything added would be both unnecessary and a compliance risk of its own.

## Prohibited patterns

Inherited from JOB-0001: no padding, no undated claims, no fabricated examples, no legal-advice framing, no urgency language beyond the cited facts. Plus, specific to this asset: **no implication that following the workbook guarantees compliance.** It structures the decision; it does not make it.
